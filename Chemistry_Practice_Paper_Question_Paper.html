AQA GCSE Chemistry Practice Paper

Topics: Atomic Structure, Bonding, Quantitative Chemistry, Chemical Changes & Energy Changes

Time allowed: 1 hour 30 minutes

Total marks: 80

Section A: Atomic Structure & Periodic Table (20 marks)

Question 1

(a) Complete the table to show the relative mass and relative charge of the three sub-atomic particles. [3 marks]

Particle Relative Mass Relative Charge
Proton
Neutron
Electron

(b) A sodium atom has 11 protons, 11 electrons and 12 neutrons. [1 mark]

What is the mass number of this sodium atom?

(c) Sodium has the electronic structure 2, 8, 1 [1 mark]

Explain why sodium is in Group 1 of the periodic table.

Question 2

The diagram shows part of the periodic table.

Group 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 0
Period 2 Li Be B C N O F Ne
Period 3 Na Mg Al Si P S Cl Ar

(a) What is the meaning of the term 'period' in the periodic table? [1 mark]

(b) Chlorine has two main isotopes: chlorine-35 and chlorine-37. [2 marks]

Explain what is meant by the term 'isotopes'.

(c) The relative atomic mass of chlorine is 35.5 [1 mark]

Suggest why the relative atomic mass is not a whole number.

Question 3

The elements in Group 1 are called the alkali metals.

(a) Describe the trend in reactivity as you go down Group 1. [1 mark]

(b) Explain, in terms of electronic structure, why the reactivity increases as you go down Group 1. [3 marks]

(c) Lithium reacts with water to produce lithium hydroxide and hydrogen gas. [2 marks]

Write a balanced symbol equation for this reaction.

Question 4

The elements in Group 7 are called the halogens.

(a) State the trend in boiling point as you go down Group 7. [1 mark]

(b) Chlorine can displace bromine from potassium bromide solution. [2 marks]

Chlorine + Potassium bromide → Potassium chloride + Bromine

Explain why this displacement reaction happens.

(c) Write a balanced symbol equation for the reaction between chlorine and potassium bromide. [2 marks]

Section B: Bonding, Structure & Properties (20 marks)

Question 5

(a) Sodium chloride (NaCl) is formed by ionic bonding. [3 marks]

Describe what happens when a sodium atom reacts with a chlorine atom to form sodium chloride.

Give your answer in terms of electron transfer.

(b) Draw a dot and cross diagram to show the bonding in sodium chloride. [2 marks]

Show only the outer electron shells.

(c) Explain why sodium chloride has a high melting point. [3 marks]

Question 6

Diamond and graphite are both forms of carbon.

(a) What name is given to different forms of the same element? [1 mark]

(b) Diamond is very hard and has a very high melting point. [3 marks]

Explain why diamond has these properties.

(c) Graphite can conduct electricity but diamond cannot. [2 marks]

Explain why graphite can conduct electricity.

Question 7

Water (H₂O) contains covalent bonds.

(a) What is a covalent bond? [1 mark]

(b) Draw a dot and cross diagram to show the bonding in a water molecule. [2 marks]

Show only the outer electron shells.

(c) Explain why water has a low boiling point compared to sodium chloride. [3 marks]

Section C: Quantitative Chemistry (20 marks)

Question 8

(a) Calculate the relative formula mass (Mr) of calcium carbonate (CaCO₃). [2 marks]

[Relative atomic masses: Ca = 40, C = 12, O = 16]

(b) Calculate the percentage by mass of calcium in calcium carbonate (CaCO₃). [2 marks]

Question 9

A student heated 2.4 g of magnesium in air to produce magnesium oxide.

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

[Relative atomic masses: Mg = 24, O = 16]

(a) Calculate the number of moles of magnesium used. [2 marks]

(b) Calculate the mass of magnesium oxide produced. [3 marks]

Question 10

In an experiment, 25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution was neutralised by 20.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid.

NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O

(a) Calculate the number of moles of hydrochloric acid used. [2 marks]

(b) Use your answer to part (a) to calculate the concentration of the sodium hydroxide solution in mol/dm³. [3 marks]

Question 11

A student burned a piece of magnesium ribbon in a crucible.

(a) The mass of magnesium used was 0.60 g [1 mark]

The mass of magnesium oxide produced was 0.95 g

Calculate the mass of oxygen that combined with the magnesium.

(b) Suggest one reason why the student's result might not be accurate. [1 mark]

(c) State the law of conservation of mass. [1 mark]

(d) Explain why, in some reactions, the mass of the reaction vessel and contents might appear to increase during a reaction. [2 marks]

Section D: Chemical Changes (10 marks)

Question 12

Metals can be arranged in order of their reactivity.

(a) Name the process used to extract metals that are less reactive than carbon. [1 mark]

(b) Iron is extracted from iron oxide in a blast furnace using carbon. [2 marks]

Iron oxide + Carbon → Iron + Carbon dioxide

Explain why this reaction is called a reduction reaction.

(c) Aluminium is more reactive than carbon. [2 marks]

Explain how aluminium is extracted from its ore.

Question 13

(a) What is meant by the term 'oxidation'? [1 mark]

(b) Magnesium reacts with copper sulfate solution. [4 marks]

Mg(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → MgSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)

Explain, in terms of oxidation and reduction, what happens to the magnesium and copper in this reaction.

Question 14

When hydrochloric acid reacts with sodium hydroxide, the temperature of the solution increases.

(a) Is this reaction exothermic or endothermic? [1 mark]

(b) Explain your answer to part (a) in terms of energy transfer. [2 marks]

(c) Draw a reaction profile diagram for this reaction. [4 marks]

Label the:

  • reactants
  • products
  • activation energy
  • energy change

Question 15

A student investigated the temperature change when different masses of ammonium nitrate were dissolved in water.

(a) The temperature of the water decreased when the ammonium nitrate dissolved. [1 mark]

What type of reaction is this?

(b) Suggest why dissolving ammonium nitrate in water causes the temperature to decrease. [2 marks]
